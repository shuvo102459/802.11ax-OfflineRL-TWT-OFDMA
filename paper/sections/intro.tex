\section{Introduction}

Wi-Fi now carries a large share of indoor traffic, and much of it originates
from battery-powered devices. IEEE 802.11ax addresses their energy cost with
two mechanisms. Target Wake Time (TWT) lets an access point schedule when each
station wakes, so radios sleep between service
periods~\cite{chen2019twt,qiu2021category}. Uplink OFDMA replaces contention
with scheduled resource units, so several stations transmit at
once~\cite{kotagiri2021distributed,han2024multiagent}. Both reduce energy, and
recent work treats their configuration as a scheduling problem in its own
right~\cite{busacca2026twt,liu2025priority}.

The two mechanisms are normally configured in isolation. TWT studies fix the
OFDMA setting, and OFDMA studies fix or ignore the sleep
schedule~\cite{karaca2020joint,noh2024joint,agbeve2025a2p}. This leaves the
joint setting unexamined, which matters because the mechanisms compete. A
trigger frame is only useful to a station that is awake to receive it, so every
OFDMA round draws on the airtime and the radio states that TWT is trying to
conserve.

A single operating point also cannot serve a real deployment. Modern basic
service sets carry mixed traffic---latency-sensitive video, periodic telemetry
and voice in one cell~\cite{yang2021twt,tan2024drl}---and the best schedule
under light telemetry is wasteful under a video burst. A controller must adapt,
which raises how it should be obtained.

Two obvious answers sit at opposite extremes. A hand-coded rule is cheap to run
but needs an expert to tune, and it is unlikely to be optimal away from the
conditions it was tuned for. An idealised online learner could in principle
track the optimum instant by instant, but it must explore on the live network,
and exploration costs latency and risks service
degradation~\cite{wydmanski2021contention,yang2024offline}. Neither extreme is
satisfactory for a deployed access point.

Offline reinforcement learning occupies the space between
them~\cite{levine2020offline,prudencio2024survey}. A policy is trained from an
already-collected dataset and never acts on the network while learning, so it
needs neither exploration nor an expert to label the right action. The price is
distribution shift, for which conservative value estimation is the standard
remedy~\cite{kumar2020cql,kostrikov2022iql}. Its wireless applications
concentrate on cellular resource
management~\cite{yang2024offline,eldeeb2026marl}, leaving Wi-Fi power save
largely unexamined.

We apply it to joint TWT and OFDMA control. Once per decision window the access
point selects a TWT schedule, a station-to-group assignment, and whether uplink
OFDMA is enabled---a 760-way discrete action. Making the OFDMA bit part of the
action, rather than a property of the run, is what makes the problem joint: the
policy can enable the scheduler when it pays and disable it when it does not.
Five offline algorithms are trained on $100{,}235$ transitions logged in ns-3.44
and compared against hand-designed and non-adaptive baselines.

Our contributions are the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item A learned policy that cuts station energy by $35.6\,\%$ against a tuned
hand-designed controller at higher throughput, obtained with no online
interaction and no expert to set its thresholds. It holds across ten independent
training runs, and separately on the seeds whose offered load steps mid-episode
and on those whose load is constant.
\item Evidence that uplink OFDMA and TWT act as substitutes rather than
complements at this operating point: enabling OFDMA raises energy at every TWT
configuration tested, and five independently trained policies order by energy
exactly as they order by how often each enables it.
\item A service-period-aligned trigger scheduler that solicits a station only
while its TWT service period can still contain the whole exchange. It removes a
race in which a solicited station sleeps before replying, leaving the access
point waiting on a radio that has switched off, and it halves the measured cost
of uplink OFDMA.
\end{itemize}
