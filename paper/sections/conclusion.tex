\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We posed TWT scheduling, station grouping and uplink OFDMA enablement as one
$760$-way offline control problem and compared five offline algorithms against
nine non-learned scenarios on fifty paired seeds.

In this setting IQL reduces station energy by $35.6\,\%$ against a tuned
hand-designed controller at higher throughput, consistently over ten training
runs, and no fixed schedule tested reaches its operating point. The
result is about IQL rather than offline RL: four of five methods lose to that
controller, and the two CQL variants match it only under retrospective
selection, so checkpoint selection rather than policy optimisation is the open
difficulty.
Under the same conditions, enabling the evaluated uplink scheduler raised
energy at every TWT configuration tested, and both a learned policy and an
independent coordinate search disable it when free to choose.

Four limits bound these claims. The topology is one basic service set, sixteen
stations and $20$\,MHz, with at most four stations solicited per trigger
against the nine resource units the channel admits; we tested four and nine and
the result held at both, but not beyond them. The station mix and traffic model
are identical across every arm, so comparisons are internally controlled but
not shown to transfer. The energy model leaves CCA-busy at ns-3's default
$273$\,mA against its own $66$\,mA receive current, an internally inconsistent
parameterisation; re-evaluating at $66$\,mA moves arm energies by $21$ to
$27\,\%$ unevenly yet changes no ordering, though the policies were trained
under the shipped profile. Finally the logged actions were held for ten
decisions, and deploying at that cadence changes energy by $-1.0\,\%$
($p = 0.12$).

Whether the substitution reverses at higher station counts, wider channels and
larger solicitation sets is the natural next step, alongside a sourced power
profile and a sweep of the grouping dimension.
