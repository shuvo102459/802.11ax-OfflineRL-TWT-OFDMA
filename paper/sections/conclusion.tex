\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

TWT scheduling, station grouping and uplink OFDMA enablement form one $760$-way
offline control problem. We trained five offline algorithms on it and measured
them against nine non-learned scenarios on fifty paired seeds.

In this setting IQL reduces station energy by $35.6\,\%$ against a tuned
hand-designed controller at higher throughput, consistently over ten training
runs, and no fixed schedule tested reaches its operating point. The
result is about IQL rather than offline RL: four of five methods lose to that
controller, and the two CQL variants match it only under retrospective
selection, so checkpoint selection rather than policy optimisation is the open
difficulty.
Under the same conditions, enabling the evaluated uplink scheduler raised
energy at every TWT configuration tested, and both a learned policy and an
independent coordinate search disable it when free to choose.

Four limits bound these claims. The topology is one basic service set, sixteen
stations and $20$\,MHz, soliciting at most four stations per trigger against
nine resource units; we tested four and nine, not beyond. Station mix and
traffic model are identical across every arm, so comparisons are internally
controlled but not shown to transfer. The energy model leaves CCA-busy at ns-3's
default $273$\,mA against its own $66$\,mA receive current; re-evaluating at
$66$\,mA moves energies $21$--$27\,\%$ unevenly yet changes no ordering.
Finally the logged actions were held for ten decisions and collected before
service-period alignment, so training and deployment differ on the OFDMA-on
half of the data.

Higher station counts, wider channels and larger solicitation sets could still
reverse the substitution; testing that needs a sourced power profile and a
sweep of the grouping dimension.
