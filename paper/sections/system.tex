\section{System Model}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Network and traffic}

We consider a single basic service set: one access point and $N = 16$ stations
on a 20\,MHz 802.11ax channel, with a beacon interval of
$T_{\mathrm{BI}} = 102.4$\,ms. All traffic is uplink.

Stations belong to four device classes, four stations each, chosen to represent
the mixed traffic of a real deployment: a low-rate IoT sensor, a video camera, a
voice assistant and a video streamer. Their offered IP-layer loads are
$0.058$, $2.037$, $0.030$ and $3.400$\,Mb/s respectively, giving
$22.1$\,Mb/s in aggregate. The access point acts once every
$\Delta = 20$ beacon intervals, or $2.048$\,s, giving $42$ decisions per
episode.

\subsection{Target Wake Time}

A TWT schedule partitions the beacon interval into $K$ service periods of
durations $\{w_1, \dots, w_K\}$. Each station is assigned to one group, and
group $k$ is awake from $\sum_{j<k} w_j$ to $\sum_{j \le k} w_j$ within every
beacon interval and sleeps outside it. Staggering groups means fewer stations
contend at once, at the cost of a shorter window each. We use a catalogue of
$20$ schedules spanning one to four service periods and $20$--$90$\,ms of total
wake time, with $19$ rules mapping the $16$ stations to groups.

\subsection{Uplink OFDMA}

When uplink OFDMA is enabled, the access point sends a trigger frame naming a
set of stations and the resource unit each may use; those stations reply
simultaneously in a trigger-based PPDU. Contention is replaced by scheduling, at
a cost: a solicited station must be awake for the trigger and stay awake through
its reply and the acknowledgement.

This is where the two mechanisms meet. A station outside its service period is
asleep and cannot be solicited, so uplink OFDMA multiplexes only \emph{within}
the currently awake group. The schedule bounds how much multiplexing is
available, and the exchange consumes part of the window it was keeping short.

The degree of multiplexing matters to what follows. The scheduler solicits at
most four stations per trigger, ns-3's default, while a $20$\,MHz channel admits
nine $26$-tone resource units. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports both limits.

\subsection{Control problem}

At decision $t$ the access point observes a state $s_t$ and selects
\begin{equation}
a_t = b_t \cdot 380 + s_t^{\mathrm{sched}} \cdot 19 + g_t,
\label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
with $s^{\mathrm{sched}} \in \{0,\dots,19\}$ a schedule, $g \in \{0,\dots,18\}$
an assignment and $b \in \{0,1\}$ enabling uplink OFDMA for that window: $760$
joint actions. The objective is to minimise station energy without reducing
delivered throughput.
