\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{State and reward}

The state is $2 + 16\times 8 = 130$-dimensional: total throughput and a
proportional-fairness index, then per station throughput, normalised backlog,
three buffer-occupancy indicators, energy efficiency, drops and energy drawn.
Rates and indicators are window statistics, counts are deltas over the
preceding window. No clock is included, though backlog correlates with elapsed
time.

The reward combines a per-station term and a fairness term. Each station
contributes $r_i$, a weighted sum of $\tanh$-scaled throughput, energy
efficiency and energy, a bonus for low buffer occupancy and a duty-cycle
penalty; $r_{\mathrm{sum}} = \sum_i r_i$ over stations active in the window
and $r_{\mathrm{fair}}$ is Jain's index over $\{r_i\}$. Energy enters
separately and explicitly, replacing the saturated term we inherited:
\begin{equation}
r = 0.30\,z\!\left(-\tanh\frac{E}{E^{*}}\right) + 0.55\,z(r_{\mathrm{sum}})
    + 0.15\,z(r_{\mathrm{fair}}),
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
where $E$ is the per-decision energy summed over stations, $E^{*}=8000$\,mJ,
and $z(\cdot)$ subtracts the mean and divides by the standard deviation of that
component on the training split alone. This is a weighted surrogate, not a
constrained optimisation: throughput is an evaluation criterion, not a
guaranteed floor on the learned policy.

\subsection{Dataset and training}

We log $2{,}404$ episodes, $100{,}235$ transitions (Alg.~\ref{alg:pipeline}).
The ten-decision hold is deliberate: with a fresh draw each decision, measured
energy is flat against schedule tightness---only a sustained schedule makes the
gradient visible. Uniform sampling gives broad nominal coverage but does not concentrate data on
high-performing trajectories---why behaviour cloning finishes last here. Splits are by episode, never by transition: $2{,}043$ training, $361$
held out. Episodes may truncate when the simulator ends a run---$83$ of
$2{,}404$ carry fewer than $42$ transitions---and truncated states are
bootstrapped, not treated as terminal. We train behaviour cloning, DQN+CQL,
Double DQN, dueling DQN+CQL and
IQL~\cite{kumar2020cql,vanhasselt2016ddqn,kostrikov2022iql} at ten
initialisations each. Ours is the discrete-action IQL: expectile regression for
$V_\psi$ and the SARSA-style target $r + \gamma V_\psi(s')$ for $Q_\theta$
as in~\cite{kostrikov2022iql}, but with the policy extracted by
$\arg\max_a Q$ rather than by advantage-weighted cloning, which a finite
action set makes unnecessary.

\subsection{Checkpoint selection}

Choosing which checkpoint to deploy is harder than it looks when the network is
off limits. Importance sampling is weak: the logger is uniform only at the ten-step refresh
points, so the per-transition propensity is not $1/760$ and a match count does
not characterise the effective sample size. Either way the overlap with a
deterministic policy is small---tens of matching pairs among $15{,}025$
held-out transitions. We rank by fitted Q evaluation~\cite{le2019fqe} on
held-out episode starts and validate that ranking against the simulator.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Offline joint TWT/OFDMA control}
\label{alg:pipeline}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Collect} $\mathcal{D}$: for each episode, draw $a \sim \mathrm{Unif}(760)$,
       hold it for $H=10$ decisions, log $(s,a,r,s')$
\STATE \textbf{Split} $\mathcal{D}$ by episode into $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}, \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ho}}$
\FOR{each algorithm $m$ and seed $\sigma \in \{42..51\}$}
  \STATE $\theta_{m,\sigma} \leftarrow$ train $m$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$, snapshot every $50$ epochs
  \STATE $k^\star \leftarrow \arg\max_k \mathrm{FQE}(\theta^{(k)}_{m,\sigma}; \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}, \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ho}})$
         \COMMENT{no simulator}
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{Deploy} $\theta^{(k^\star)}$: each decision, take
      $a_t = \pi_m(s_t)$, decode $(b_t, s^{\mathrm{sched}}_t, g_t)$, and if
      $b_t$ solicit only stations whose service period can still hold a whole
      exchange \COMMENT{$\pi_m$: $\arg\max_a Q$ for the value-based methods,
      $\arg\max_a \mathrm{softmax}(f_\phi(s))$ for BC}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Service-period-aligned triggering}

A trigger exchange started near the end of a service period cannot finish inside
it. The station sleeps before it replies, and the access point is left awaiting
a reply from a radio that has switched off. That is a protocol fault, not a
missed transmission opportunity.

We therefore solicit a station only while its service period still has room for
the entire exchange, which we take as $5.8$\,ms, covering the trigger, two short
interframe spaces, a maximum-duration trigger-based PPDU and the acknowledgement.
This aligns uplink OFDMA with the awake window by construction.
